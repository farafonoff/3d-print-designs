# vibe-design: working notes

Parametric 3D-printable designs in OpenSCAD (the user may write "openscada"). One folder per design. The `.scad` files are the primary artifact; STLs are exports and may be stale. Remote: `git@github.com:farafonoff/3d-print-designs.git`.

## Conventions
- Parameters live at the top of the main `.scad`, grouped in `// ---------- Section ----------` blocks. Mark measurements the user still has to verify with `MEASURE` (see the Customizer rule below).
- Customizer syntax: OpenSCAD's Customizer reads a trailing `// [...]` comment on a parameter line as an enum or range (`// [a, b, c]` dropdown, `// [0:10]` slider), so a note like `// [MEASURE] pipe outer diameter` becomes a one-item dropdown. Write ordinary notes as inline block comments, `d = 7; /* MEASURE: pipe outer diameter */`, and use the bracket form only for real option lists, as widely as possible: `layout = "assembled"; // [print, hook, holder, dish, assembled, check]`, likewise for any parameter with a fixed set of values. Existing files still use `// [MEASURE]` and the long `// print / hook / ...` layout comments; convert them when touching the file (soapholder first).
- `test_*.scad` files `use <main.scad>` and intersect `grip()`-style modules with a box to cut a small test piece. They must not override parameters; tuning happens only in the main file.
- Each `.scad` starts with a leading `//` comment block describing it; keep it current, `previews.sh` copies it into the folder README. Each project folder has `previews.sh` (renders every `.scad` except `test*.scad` and `lib*.scad` (helper libraries) to `previews/` and refreshes the generated section of the folder's `README.md`). The `previews.sh` scripts are identical copies: change all of them. `*.gcode` is git-ignored.
- The user checks designs visually in OpenSCAD. Don't render or open previews unless asked, except to validate a script or fix an error they pasted.
- Paste of OpenSCAD console output usually means "fix this". "No top level geometry" means the final module call is missing.
- Commit or push only when asked. Commit trailer: see the session attribution reminder.

## bikegrips (TPU lock-on grips, sleeve only)
User reuses the metal lock rings from a worn-out lock-on grip and prints only the rubber sleeve in TPU. `lockon_grip.scad` is the whole design; `test_grip.scad` prints the bottom 11 mm (lips + one hex row) to check fit.

Caliper measurements of the original (user bought a caliper, replaces earlier photo estimates):
- Bore 22.2-22.3 mm on the original; modelled as 22.2 (`fit_clear` 0) for a snug TPU fit, tune by 0.1 after the test print.
- Lips: outer diameter 26.3 mm, width 16.3 mm between the two cutting planes (a metric width, not an angle), depth 8 mm (= width of the metal ring; 7.2 was a first caliper reading of the old rubber). Two opposite lips with straight, parallel sides, made by two rectangular cuts.
- Pattern starts exactly where the lips end, so seat length equals lip depth.
- Fat variant now the default: `body_d = 33` at the ends, `bulge = 0.5` (34 mm at centre). The original grip measured 29 mm; set `body_d = 29`, `bulge = 1.0` for that. The metal ring is 33 mm OD (`ring_od`); when `body_d < ring_od` a solid `flare_len` cone (default 3 mm) joins them and the pattern starts after it, with `body_d >= ring_od` there is no flare.
- Thick part outer diameter 29 mm of the original (modelled as the diameter at the ends of the pattern; `bulge` adds a smooth sine-shaped swell in the middle, default +1 mm radius so ~31 mm at centre; pockets and logo engraving follow it via `body_r()` / `envelope()`; `bulge = 0` gives the straight cylinder). Cross-section oval was discussed and declined: the user meant a barrel profile along the length. The wall under the pockets is then 2.15 mm (`wall_min` lowered to 2.0).
- Pattern: hex pockets, inverted (recessed with raised ridges), deep so it wears slowly. Started at 2.6 mm cells (hex_r 1.5), scaled 1.5x at the user's request (hex_r 2.25, ~3.9 mm across flats) with the same 1.2 mm depth and ~10% wider ridges (0.88 mm).
- The earlier 15 mm lip print (25.5 mm seat) was visibly too small in the ring; not yet tested on a bar.

OpenSCAD gotcha: a top-level variable cannot reference one defined further down the file (it becomes undef), so define the source value first.

Mistakes not to repeat: the lip cuts were misread several times (sector wedges, parallel slab, "+" slots). The correct reading is two cuts at 90 degrees to the lips.

Logos: Claude and OpenAI marks (simple-icons SVGs in `logos/`) are engraved into the hex pattern (no big flat pads) at 80% of the pocket depth, with a 1.2 mm solid border that follows the logo outline and fills the pockets around it. Trademarks, personal use only.

## bookmarks (image-based, for the user's wife, printed in PLA)
Sources are black/white artwork in `source/` (`cat_pattern.jpeg`, `fish_pattern.jpeg`); white = material, black = cut through. `./prep_images.sh` (ImageMagick) thresholds, crops and downsizes them into `build/<name>.png` (mask) and `build/<name>_h.png` (height profile with a rounded `EDGE_R` = 0.3 mm fillet ramp from the blurred mask); both are committed so previews work. The size table at the bottom of `prep_images.sh` (h, t per bookmark) must match `h` and `t` in `<name>_bookmark.scad`.
`lib_bookmark.scad`: `bookmark()` builds the top half with `surface()` from the `_h` map, mirrors it for the bottom (rounded edges in cross-section on both faces), intersects with an extrusion of the artwork footprint traced from the same map (otherwise black pixels leave a paper-thin film), and scales everything to w x h x t. `bookmark_flat()` is the plain 2D extrusion with optional `smooth` corner rounding. Verified with a cross-section slice. Thin lace lines are about 0.7 mm at 36 mm width; PLA is brittle there. The fillet is only ~1.5 px wide at PX_H=900, so it reads as a small chamfer; raise `PX_H` for a rounder one (slower render). No potrace installed; the user is fine with the `surface()` route and with the anti-aliasing (greyscale) interpretation.
- `bikegrips/barend.scad`: two-piece expanding bar-end plug, remade from `Barend.stl` (a Thingiverse part, kept next to it for reference; author/licence not recorded, check before redistributing). One file, `layout` = print / sleeve / wedge / assembled, `section = true` cuts it open. Sleeve = 33 mm head (5 mm tall, half the original, so the plug could grow to 21 mm for longer petals) + plug (18.6 mm) cut by crossed slits (`slit_planes = 2`, four petals; 1 = one slit) with a conical bore (6 deg half-angle); wedge = cone with the M6 nut in a closed cavity loaded from the side (45 deg slot, back wall behind it so unscrewing drives the nut against the wall and pushes the wedge out), one fin per slit stops it spinning. Screw M6x30 countersunk goes in through the head: 90 deg cone seat 11.6 mm at the face (0.1 sink), straight 6.2 mm hole (bolt_clear 0.2). The user fixed the layout: bolt in the thick head, nut in the thin plug. An earlier reading of the STL section put the hex in the head; that was wrong. Not yet printed or test-fitted: plug diameter vs the tube bore, wedge travel (9 mm, about 0.75 mm radial spread), side-loaded nut cavity (wall behind the nut corners is only ~0.7 mm, PETG), wedge wall around the nut.
- `bikegrips/barend_onepiece.scad`: simpler fallback, one piece with a tapered hex nut pocket (8 mm) in the plug tip.

## doorcable
- `door_cable_holder.scad` (mk1) is frozen: strip standing on edge, channel opens sideways, tape recess on a plate. `door_cable_holder_mk2.scad` is the simplified open-top version (flat base, lip, thick walls; part height = gap_h - tape_t - clearance). The user wants mk1 left unchanged. `*.json` hold the user's customizer parameter sets (`frontdoor`, `frontdoor_08`); mk2's JSON is an exact copy of mk1's, so a name shared with mk1 (e.g. `lip_t`) is overridden silently: give new mk2 parameters their own names (`lip_thick`).
- Bend extents, `mid_len` and `min_width` are measured wall to wall (faces towards the inside of the U); centre-line values add `wall_in`. Bends are quarter ellipses swept as short straight pieces with overlap.
- The `doorcable/*.stl` files are huge (up to 50 MB) and git-ignored.
- Do not change anything the user has not asked for (an unrequested change to mk1 drew a complaint). When they report a problem, ask or offer options first. When told to clone something "exactly", copy it and diff the bounding boxes.

## powerbank
- `powerbank_clip.scad`: sleeve that slides over a 77 x 22.7 mm power bank (cavity = exact size, `fit = 0`; the user wants it tight, negative = stretch). Z, bottom to top: three USB-C clips (plugs point down), wrap zone with a comb of cable channels on each side wall (6 turns, 4 mm cable, 3 mm opening), three USB-A clips (plugs point up). Clips in a row share their walls.
- Printed standing up with no supports, so no feature may overhang: the flanges were removed (unprintable), comb walls and openings slope up at `comb_angle` = 45 degrees (channel hole keeps its round/teardrop shape), the USB-A clips start with a 45 degree ramp. Check every new feature for floating regions.
- A stale STL can come from OpenSCAD's own editor buffer or Customizer (the user once measured 77.4 from `fit = 0.2` while the file said 0): check the STL's vertices before blaming the model.

## soapholder
- `soap_holder.scad`: two identical S-hooks for a 7 mm pipe (bore 7.1, 16 mm wide, 60 mm apart, printed lying on their side, no supports) with a 5 mm shelf under the dish and one long zigzag rib (40 mm front-back, swing +-3.5 mm along the pipe) on the shelf top; the dish is `Soap_Dish.stl` (third-party, author/licence not recorded, 2 MB, never read it as text) sitting on a 4 mm flat plate that follows its bottom outline (rounded rectangle 90.4 x 60.4) unioned with it, with a matching zigzag cut through the plate above each hook (blind, stops at the dish floor). The plate is the first layers when the dish is printed bottom-down, so no supports. The user's rules: hooks horizontal, no supports, never modify the STL, only add to it with `union`. Rejected attempts: separate dovetail holder (too heavy, held nothing), round legs under the dish (unprintable), small tabs sticking out past the dish ends (the user did not see the zigzag in the dish; they want a plate following the bottom, visibly part of the dish). `layout` = print / hook / dish / assembled / check (`check` shows only the flat plate-on-shelf contact when nothing overlaps). `print` only lays out the hooks.
- The STL is binary, Y is its depth axis (flat back at y=-20, open towards +y), Z is its long side (tall wall at z-, low wall with hex hole at z+); its flat bottom is 90.4 mm along the pipe (half 45.2) and 60.4 mm front-back, its walls flare outwards. It stands flat-down on the shelves with the tall wall at the back (by the stem) and the low wall in front (the user turned it around once); the `dish_*` parameters were measured from it at its centre line. Zigzag steps must stay below 45 degrees (dx per step / swing), because the hook prints on its side. Not printed yet: rib fit (`dish_tol` 0.4 per side, on the dish part only; the hook rib is the nominal size).
